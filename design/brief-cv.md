# feedMyPixel - CV document brief

**For:** [claude.ai/design](https://claude.ai/design). A printed document, not a screen. Output is a
visual brief for re-implementation, and the implementation target is unusual, so read the
constraints section before designing anything.

## Build on the locked system

The one-pager is live at <https://feedmypixel.com>, built from your bundles. Everything in `_ds/`
applies: Pixel Blue, Plus Jakarta Sans, DM Mono, the ink and surface scales, the pixel-square radii.
Ship the same `_ds/` back so the conformance check can run.

The CV and the site should read as one identity. A recruiter often has both open. Today they look
like different people made them.

## What this is

Ben's CV. It is the document that gets emailed, printed, skimmed in under a minute and forwarded. It
is linked from the hero of the site and downloaded directly, so it is a public artefact of the brand
as much as the site is.

It replaces an eleven page document that had the weight in the wrong end: three paragraphs on a 2005
QA role, two sentences on three years at DEFRA.

**Do not write the CV.** The words already exist and are supplied with this brief. They come from
Ben's own history and in places from the commit record, so invented replacements are worse than
useless, they are wrong. Use the supplied text verbatim in the render. If a passage does not fit the
layout, say so in the changelog rather than rewriting it. **The layout is what this brief asks for.**

## The hard constraint, read this first

The design will be reproduced as an **OpenDocument text file** generated by a script, opened in
LibreOffice, exported to PDF. Not a web page. That rules out a lot:

- **No CSS grid or flexbox.** Two column layouts must be expressible as a table with invisible
  borders, because that is what a word processor has.
- **No absolute positioning, no overlap, no rotation, no bleed.** Content flows top to bottom.
- **Backgrounds are hard.** A tinted band behind a section is possible but fragile across a page
  break. Prefer rules, weight and space to blocks of colour.
- **Every visual difference must map to a named paragraph or character style**, because that is what
  the generator writes. If two things look different they need different styles, and the number of
  styles should stay small enough to name.
- **Web fonts do not exist here.** Plus Jakarta Sans and DM Mono are installed locally and embedded
  into the PDF. Use only those two.
- **Absolute print units only.** Give every size in pt or mm. A word processor has no pixels and no
  root font size, so `rem` and `px` have to be guessed at and the printed result drifts from the
  design. This applies to type sizes, spacing, margins and column widths alike.

Design as if for print in 1995, then use the type system to make it feel like 2026. The constraint is
real and a design that ignores it cannot be built.

## Page and content

A4, portrait. Roughly three to four pages. Margins are yours to propose.

The document, in order:

1. **Name and identity.** Ben Chidgey, contract full-stack engineer, feedMyPixel Ltd, remote
   worldwide and UK based. Email, site, GitHub, LinkedIn. **Include the feedMyPixel logo**: he trades
   through the company and the site carries the mark. Size and placement are your call.
2. **A three line opening statement.** What he does, what with, who for.
3. **Skills**, in three bands: core, also, throughout.
4. **Experience.** Around twenty five entries, newest first, each with company, title, dates,
   location, engagement type, a paragraph and a list of technologies. The four most recent get a
   longer paragraph; older ones get one or two lines. **Every role appears.** There is no summarised
   block: the older work still shows range and continuity, and Ben wants it visible.
5. **Education.** University, and what the degree covered. Further education certificates. GCSEs
   with grades. These stay in full. They are older than the career and that is the point.
6. **References.** Available on request.

## The real design problems

1. **Twenty five entries without monotony.** The current version is a uniform stack and it reads as a
   list rather than a career. Recent work should feel weightier than a two line entry from 2007
   without either of them looking like a mistake. Density is the whole problem here.
2. **Where do dates live?** The site puts them in a narrow left column against the content. That
   reads well and shortens the document, but in a word processor it means a table per entry. Worth
   it, or is there a better arrangement for print?
3. **The technology lists.** On the site these are tags, and they look like tags. In a document they
   are a comma separated line in mono and they read as an afterthought. They carry a lot of the
   searchable value, so they deserve a treatment.
4. **Page breaks.** A role must not split from its heading, and the education block should not strand
   two lines on a final page. Show what the rhythm looks like when a section lands badly.
5. **One page or several?** Some readers want a one page summary. Is there a version of the first
   page that stands alone if nothing else is read?

## Constraints

- **Two typefaces only**, Plus Jakarta Sans and DM Mono.
- **Print legibility.** Body text no smaller than 10pt, metadata no smaller than 8.5pt. Grey text
  must survive laser printing and photocopying, so nothing lighter than mid grey on white.
- **Colour is nearly free in PDF and expensive on paper.** Assume it may be printed in black and
  white and still has to work.
- **No em dashes and no en dashes anywhere.** Plain hyphens. No serial commas.
- **British English.**

## Deliver

`_ds/` re-exported, one self-contained HTML render of the full document at A4 proportions showing
every section and at least one page break, real content rather than lorem and a short changelog.

**Also deliver a named style list**, the most useful thing in the drop, because it is what gets
implemented. One row per visual treatment: style name, font, size in pt, weight, colour, space before,
space after and line height.

**And a table list.** Anywhere two things sit side by side, name the block and give its column widths
in mm along with the total. Tables are the only side by side primitive available, so a layout without
these numbers cannot be built.

Prose describing the design is the least useful thing you can send.
