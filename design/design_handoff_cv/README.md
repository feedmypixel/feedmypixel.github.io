# feedMyPixel — CV document handoff

Ben's CV as a printed A4 document. The implementation target is an OpenDocument text file generated by a script, opened in LibreOffice, exported to PDF — so the design is built from word-processor primitives only.

## Read in this order

1. **`STYLES.md`** and **`TABLES.md`** — the named style list and the column widths. Nineteen paragraph styles, five character styles, the page master, and every side-by-side block with its column widths in millimetres. All sizes are absolute print units, points and millimetres, with no relative units anywhere. These two files are what gets implemented and they are the most useful things in the drop.
2. **`render/CV.dc.html`** — the working document at true A4 proportions, all four pages, real content. Open it directly with `doc-page.js` and `support.js` alongside.
3. **`_ds/tokens.css`** — the site's tokens, unchanged, plus a **PRINT-SUBSET** note listing which are print-safe and which are not.
4. **`CHANGELOG.md`**.

## The copy is supplied, not written

**The render lays out Ben's words. It does not generate them.** Every passage is verbatim from the supplied CV draft, which is the source of truth. Three small departures exist, all on Ben's spoken instruction and all listed in `CHANGELOG.md`; nothing is invented and nothing is paraphrased.

A provenance check is recorded in `CHANGELOG.md` and is worth repeating after any change: the rendered text must still contain the specifics only Ben's history holds (Capacitor, the GDS delivery figures, multi-file upload, Mobile Profile HTML, three people to over 150) and must contain none of the generic vocabulary that invented copy reaches for (spearheaded, cross-functional, leveraged). If that check ever flips, the words are being written rather than laid out.


Every word is Ben's, taken verbatim from `src/lib/data/experience.ts`, `tasks/cv-paste.txt` and the text layer of the old `static/BenChidgeyCV.pdf`. Where a supplied passage conflicts with the house style, it is **flagged in `CHANGELOG.md` and left alone** rather than rewritten. Three such conflicts exist, all in the education block. Nothing here is invented.

## What was designed against the ODT constraint

No CSS grid or flexbox in the intent. No absolute positioning, no overlap, no rotation, no tinted bands. Every visual difference maps to a named style. Two typefaces. The render uses flex for the two right-aligned rows because HTML has no tab stops — **those are tab stops in the document**, and `STYLES.md` says so per style. There are no tables in the design at all.

## Answers to the five design problems

**Twenty four entries without monotony.** Density is one property varied three ways: space before the company name, 14 pt for the four most recent down to 11 pt for the oldest, working with copy that is already graded in length. No size, colour or rule changes between tiers, so a short entry reads as brief rather than broken. A 2005 freelance entry is three lines and a 2023 government entry is nine, and neither looks like a mistake.

**Where dates live.** On a right-aligned tab stop at the right margin, on the same line as the company name. Not a narrow left column, because in a word processor that means a table per entry — eighteen fragile objects that resist restyling and complicate page breaks. One paragraph, one style, no table.

**The technology lines.** A 2 pt Pixel Blue left paragraph border with 8 pt padding. Native word-processor primitive, survives page breaks, prints as a legible grey rule in black and white, and echoes the spine marker on the site's Experience section so the two artefacts read as one identity.

**Page breaks.** `SectionHead`, `RoleHead*` and `RoleTitle` carry keep-with-next, so a company name is never the last line on a page and a heading never strands from its entry. The render is explicitly paginated to show real break positions; every page has 73 to 248 pt of unused tail, so a natural flow lands close to the same breaks.

**One page or several.** Page one stands alone: identity, opening statement, skills, and the three most recent roles including the three years at DEFRA. A reader who stops there has the whole current picture. That is why the page one break falls after Wayfair rather than being balanced by length.

## Flagged: things the site should change

- **Email.** The CV prints `ben@feedmypixel.com`, because a CV exists to get a reply and Ben cannot send from `hello@`. The site still shows `hello@` in the hero pill, the contact aside and the footer. They should match, and `ben@` is the one that can answer.
- **`--ink-subtle` is not print-safe.** `#5d6b80` is below the brief's mid-grey floor and does not survive photocopying. The CV uses `--ink-muted` `#4a586b` everywhere the site would use subtle. Worth knowing before any other printed artefact reuses the token.
- **Section heads are sentence case, not caps.** `tasks/cv-paste.txt` has SKILLS, EXPERIENCE, EDUCATION in capitals; that is plain-text formatting, not a typographic decision, and the locked system forbids ALL-CAPS. Rendered as Skills, Experience, Education, References.
- **DEFRA database.** An earlier draft of the copy said the .NET service templates used Postgres. They defaulted to MongoDB, with Postgres available on request. The CV now names neither in the prose — a CV does not need it — and carries MongoDB in the technology line. The same error is still in `tasks/cv-paste.txt` and `tasks/cv-draft.md`; the shipped site data is clean.
